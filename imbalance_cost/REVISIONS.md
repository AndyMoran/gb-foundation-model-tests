# Imbalance exposure: revisions

Choices made after the plan was tagged `imbalance-cost-plan` (f0678fa). None changes a forecaster, a measure, a set, a label rule or a headline.

1. **How a live calendar forecast proves its inputs** (`gb-weather-private/weather/calendar_live.py`, PLAN.md §6). Demand: the INDO series is cut at 08:30 UK time on D−1 from the prospective run's cache, whose values are never overwritten, and its SHA-256 must equal the `input_sha256` the prospective run recorded for D when it forecast D. A day whose hash differs is refused, not forecast. Weather: the committed, unchanged `weather_study/inputs/<D>.csv` pushed for D. Calendar: E5's function and the hashed bank-holiday file. Each day's record lists every hash and the commit of the weather file.
2. **Order of the daily runs.** The calendar forecast for D needs the prospective record for D (made at 09:45) and the weather inputs for D (pushed from 10:00), so it runs after both: suggested at 10:30, 16:00 and 21:30 UK time. A run that finds either missing stops and leaves the day to the next run.
3. **The reproduction check** reruns E5's arm A on six backtest days (every 160th of the 960) and requires every half-hour within 1 MW of `weather_study/results/calendar_forecasts.csv`. It is run once, committed, and required by every later run of the same script. It confirms the code path; nothing is tuned on it.
4. **Weights.** Chronos-2's weights are checked against the prospective lock, the same pinned revision the no-weather live run uses.
5. **One day per call,** for every live and reconstructed day, although Chronos-2 runs with `cross_learning = False` and batching would not change its forecasts.
6. **Reading the API's format.** To write `fetch_prices.py`, two sample responses were opened on 23 November 2026, after the tag: the System Price for 15 January 2025 (its first two periods were shown) and the Market Index Data for four records of the same morning. They were read to confirm field names and units, not analysed, and no exposure was computed. This is the only price seen before the data step.
7. **Which settlement run.** PLAN.md §7 asks for the settlement run of every System Price. The open endpoint (`/balancing/settlement/system-prices`, dataset DISEBSP) does not name the run. The script records each period's `createdDateTime` and the dataset instead, and the note will say that the run cannot be named from open data.
8. **Where the Market Index Price is built.** `fetch_prices.py` builds it (the volume-weighted average of providers with volume above 0; missing when none has volume), so the built file is hashed with the raw files. A provider whose volume is 0 is never averaged in.
9. **Stage 1 only, for now.** `score.py` scores stage 1. Loading the live forecasts for stage 2 needs files that do not exist yet (the weather arm is made after the window), so that loader is added before stage 2 runs, recorded here first, with every measure and rule unchanged (PLAN.md §12).
10. **Pooled measures.** "Per MWh of error" and the effective ratio are ratios of totals over the days of a set, as their definitions in §8 require; each draw recomputes both totals. A draw in which the surplus cost per MWh is not positive leaves the ratio undefined for that draw, and the count of such draws is reported.
11. **The evening sets** use the window's own demand: net exposure in the evening is £ per GWh of demand between 16:00 and 19:30.
12. **The synthetic independent forecaster** is built so that, within each day, it is short and long equally often in each price state. Its exposure is then exactly 0, a stricter test than "near 0" (§9.4). A first version drew its signs at random; one seed put the interval just above 0, which is what a 90% interval does one time in ten, so the construction was made exact before any real run.
13. **Check 1** compares each forecaster's mean daily error over all 960 days, before any day is dropped for a missing price, with the arbitrage study's; the weather-only Chronos-2 bridge is not in that study and is reported only. The headline's figures are whole pounds per GWh.
14. **Frequency and tail, added after the stage 1 results** (`explore.py`). A review pointed out that the mean net exposure answers one question, the average net cost, and cannot show how often a forecaster made costly misses or how bad the worst were. `explore.py` adds, descriptively: the share of half-hours on the costly side; how concentrated gross losses are in each forecaster's worst half-hours; behaviour in the top tenth of half-hours by price stress; tail percentiles and the worst days; a demand-weighted average beside the day-weighted one; and the winter share of losses, which scores the one prediction `score.py` did not measure. It was written after the results were seen, is committed before it runs, and changes nothing in `score.py` or its results. The draft note's sentence "so a miss is about as likely to earn as to cost" was removed before publication, as only amounts had been measured.
15. **A code review of `explore.py`, after its run** (24 November 2026). None of its points changes a figure in `explore_results.json`. Two were raised as possible crashes and checked against the code and the run: `score.label` already returns "not clearly different" when an interval has no finite draws, and the day labels are plain strings, so the JSON write succeeded. The script is not edited. Before stage 2 reuses `score.py` or `explore.py`, these changes are made and recorded here: an interval with no finite draws is labelled "undefined", not "not clearly different"; the synthetic winter-share check divides by gross losses, as the measure does, not by net exposure; the real run checks that its scored days equal `score.py`'s as a set, not only in number, and checks before writing any output; bootstrap half-hours are built one draw at a time, not all at once; the synthetic gate uses fewer draws; every JSON write uses `default=str` and every file is opened with `with`; and `q90` is renamed for what it returns, a 90% interval. Intervals for comparisons with the regression are over draws where both measures are finite; for stage 1 every draw was finite for every forecaster that has losses.
16. **A superseded sentence in the stage 1 note** (`RESULTS_stage1.md`, ecfd1e0). In the paragraph "A megawatt-hour of miss was worth about £2, net", the sentence "This note does not show how often a miss cost money rather than earned it, nor whether the offset comes from many small gains or a few large ones; only the amounts were measured" was written before `explore.py` ran. It is superseded by the note's own "Frequency and tail" section: misses were on the costly side in 46.6% to 47.9% of half-hours and on the earning side in 43.6% to 45.0%, and each forecaster's worst 10% of half-hours carried about two-thirds of its gross losses. No figure in the note changes. The note is not edited.
17. **A second exploratory script, `worst_days.py`** (26 November 2026), written after the stage 1 results and `explore.py`'s were seen, and committed before it runs. The question came from drafting a post: Chronos-2 had the lowest exposure of every forecaster on 8 January 2025, so why was the mean of its worst 5% of days not clearly different from the regression's? The ten worst days already in `explore_results.json` included a clock change (27 October 2024) and Easter 2026, so the script flags every scored day (clock change from its half-hour count; bank holiday and Easter from GOV.UK's list, checked against `data/fetch_manifest.json`; Christmas; the coldest tenth; weekend) and reports, for each forecaster's own worst 5% of days, which flags they carry, how much of their total comes from calendar days, the worst-5% mean without calendar days, and whether bad days came from big misses or stressed prices. It is descriptive, with no intervals or labels, and changes nothing already published. Its synthetic check and a test render of its report were run while writing it, on invented data only.
